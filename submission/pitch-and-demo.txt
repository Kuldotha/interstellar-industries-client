One sentence
A persistent planet colony builder where settlers, production chains and growing neighbourhoods run on Solana through MagicBlock.

Short description
Build a home on a small spherical planet. Feed your settlers, turn local resources into industry, and meet their growing needs. Interstellar Industries combines an atom-punk colony builder with a shared Rust simulation running in the browser and on a MagicBlock Ephemeral Rollup. Session keys keep building actions free of repeated wallet prompts. The playable devnet demo takes a colony from its first houses to farming, clothing, beer, electricity and radio.

Product pitch
Interstellar Industries is for players who enjoy making a settlement work: finding a good coastline, getting the first food supply running, and watching a handful of homes become a community.

Your first planet is a free place to learn. Housing brings workers. Workers support factories. Factories supply resources and goods that let your population grow. The challenge is balancing those connections, not clicking through wallet confirmations.

The game runs in a desktop browser. A player connects a Solana wallet, authorizes a browser session, and builds. The colony's gameplay state lives on a MagicBlock Ephemeral Rollup. Its production and population continue over time when the player leaves.

The current demo includes the first population tier, a guided tutorial, linked farm fields, utility coverage, power and storage-aware production. The longer-term direction is an interplanetary economy: a welcoming homeworld supported by specialized planets. Additional playable planets, planet purchases and tier-two gameplay are future work, not features in the current demo.

Planned business model
A free starting planet lets players establish a colony before deciding whether to expand. Purchasable additional planets are the intended source of revenue. Pricing, demand and conversion have not yet been validated.

Technical description
One deterministic Rust simulation is compiled for both WebAssembly and the Solana program. The browser uses it for immediate local predictions; authorized on-chain instructions validate actions and update the authoritative state.

Factories share planet-wide inventory and a workforce multiplier. The engine handles fractional production, competing inputs, population needs, electricity and storage caps. Full storage reduces production to the amount that can still be consumed. Positive inventory can cover a production shortfall until depletion.

The engine retains production metadata and calculates the next state boundary. Stable intervals can be advanced using their rates; scheduled callbacks process changes such as stock depletion, storage filling and population changes. It does not require every planet to run a full simulation every second.

Planet placement uses shared fixed-point generation and immutable topology. The program checks terrain and neighbouring tiles instead of trusting heights provided by the browser. A fishing dock must face water.

Wallet signatures authorize a session through a constrained signing service. Ordinary ER transactions use the browser session key. Starter planet account costs are sponsored. Starter state exists on the ER and is not settled to the base layer in this implementation.

Source is public for review, with all rights reserved. Do not describe it as open source.
